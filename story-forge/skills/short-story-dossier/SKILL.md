# Short-Story Dossier

**Role:** PRODUCER. Entry point for the short-story track. Turns a raw idea into a tightly scoped short-story dossier: the call sheet that tells every downstream stage exactly what effect to serve, what change to execute, and what each character is for. No chapter planning. No worldbuilding sprawl. The dossier for a short story fits on two pages.

**System posture for all steps:** Expert developmental editor calibrated for the compressed short form. Every output decision is governed by one question: does this serve the single unified effect? Specificity beats generality. Emotional precision beats category labels.

---

## Dependency Check

Run before any generation. Do not skip.

**Required input:** a braindump (raw dump of everything the writer knows about this idea: premise, images, characters, tone, comp titles, what to avoid) and a working title.

If either is missing, stop and ask:

> "This skill needs your braindump and a working title. Give me everything you know about this story: premise, images, characters, tone, things to avoid, comp titles. Rough is fine. What is the working title?"

Do not proceed without a braindump and a working title.

---

## Step 1: Brainstorm 3 to 5 Candidate Premises

**Goal:** Generate 3 to 5 short-story premises from the braindump. Each must be a single paragraph delivering: protagonist, their heart's desire, the problem that threatens it, and a sense of the emotional effect the finished story will produce. The inciting incident must be present or strongly implied.

**Constraints:**

- No default genre furniture. The setting and execution must feel specific to this writer's braindump, not to the category.
- Each premise must imply a compressed arc. If you cannot identify an irreversible change in the pitch, the premise is not yet a story.
- Vary the emotional register across candidates. Do not cluster them all at the same pitch.
- One paragraph each. No lists, no sub-bullets within the premise.

Run this step at high creative temperature. The goal is genuine variety across the candidates.

---

## Step 2: Select the Best Premise

**Goal:** Evaluate all candidates on four criteria. Output the winner verbatim. No preamble. No analysis.

**Evaluation criteria (apply in this order):**

1. **Poe's unity of effect.** Which premise implies the clearest, most powerful single emotional effect? A story that tries to produce two or three effects produces none of them cleanly.

2. **Rust Hills's irreversible change.** Which premise makes the irreversible change most clearly unavoidable? The change must follow from who the character is, not from circumstance alone.

3. **Heart's desire specificity.** Which protagonist has the most emotionally specific desire? "She wants to escape" is vague. "She wants her father to choose her over the bottle, once, before her wedding" is specific.

4. **Braindump fidelity.** Which best honors what the writer brought in: the specific images, characters, tone, things to avoid?

After selecting: reproduce the winning premise verbatim. Nothing else.

---

## Step 3: Build the Short-Story Dossier

**Goal:** Produce a compact, structured dossier covering every element the story needs before outlining begins. Maximum two pages. If any section exceeds its slot, trim. Depth comes in the outline and draft stages.

Provide the selected premise, the braindump, and the instructions below. Context before instructions.

**Required sections:**

**The Effect (Poe).** One sentence. What is the single emotional effect this story is designed to produce in a reader who reads it in one sitting? Choose a specific feeling: not "sadness," but "the grief of recognizing what you gave up before you were old enough to understand the cost." The effect governs every downstream decision.

**The Change (Rust Hills).** One sentence. What is the one central irreversible change? Name who changes, what changes, and what makes it irreversible. If the change is in understanding rather than circumstance, say so explicitly.

**Heart's Desire.** Protagonist's name and heart's desire in one sentence. The desire must appear on the first page of the finished story. It must be specific and emotional, not functional.

**Magic Sword.** One to two sentences: what the magic sword is (the power, ability, piece of knowledge, or ally the protagonist must gain or learn to wield to win their heart's desire), whether they hold it at the start or must acquire it, and whether they know how to use it yet. The three-beat arc (plant, development, climactic deployment) is designed in the outline stage. Name the sword here.

**Cast.** Two to six characters only. For each: name, one-sentence description, their heart's desire, and their moral-function slot. Do not exceed six characters. A short story requiring more than six named characters is carrying novel-weight cast.

Moral-function slots (use only the slots this story requires; one slot per character unless their conflict within a shared slot is the point):
- Protagonist
- Antagonist (person, system, or the protagonist's own flaw)
- Moral witness (the character positioned to see clearly at the ending)
- Overconfident operator (produces the Collision Machine escalations in ensemble crime)
- Panicker
- The one who knows by accident
- Foil (reflects what the protagonist could become if the change does not happen)

**One POV.** Name the POV character. Name the psychic distance: close (inside the character's sensory and cognitive moment) or distant (observational, report-like). Short fiction typically runs close. State the reason for this choice in one sentence.

**Conceptual Hook.** What makes this specific story worth reading to a reader picking it up cold? What does it do that no other story in the same genre doing the same premise does? One to two sentences. Not a tagline; a craft description.

**Target Word Band.** Choose one: 4,000 to 6,000 words (WotF competition sweet spot), 5,000 to 7,000 words (full short story), or 2,000 to 4,000 words (compressed piece). State the reason for the choice. Note any market or contest target.

**Genre and Tone Signal.** One sentence. What is the registered genre, and what tonal note does the opening establish? This is the promise the opening makes to the reader. For dark-comedy-crime: the comic register must be established in the opening, not introduced mid-story after the darkness has already set the register.

**Outline Plan Notes.** Bullet list only. What the scene outline will need to address: where to enter the story (apply the enter-late principle), the approximate number of scenes the word band allows, any structural difficulty in the premise, where the magic sword plants and pays off, and what makes the ending both surprising and inevitable.

---

## Step 4: Dossier Critique

**Goal:** Audit the dossier for problems before the final pass. Produce an improvement plan only. Do not rewrite.

Provide the premise, the braindump, the dossier, and the critique criteria below. Context before instructions.

**Critique criteria:**

- **Effect coherence.** Does every section serve the stated single effect? Flag any element that pulls in a different emotional direction.
- **Irreversibility.** Is the central change genuinely irreversible as stated, or could the character revert? A change that can be undone in scene two is not the change.
- **Heart's desire specificity.** Is the desire emotionally precise? If it could apply to a hundred characters in similar positions, it is too general.
- **Magic sword visibility.** Is the sword identifiable and plant-able? If it cannot be planted on the first page (even subtly), reconsider its definition.
- **Cast economy.** Does every cast member justify their slot? Is any character doing work another character already does?
- **Braindump fidelity.** Does the dossier honor the writer's stated images, tonal intentions, and things to avoid?

For each issue: name the section, describe the specific problem, give a concrete suggestion for what to change and why. Do not rewrite the dossier. Produce the improvement plan only.

---

## Step 5: Dossier Rewrite

**Goal:** Implement the critique plan. Apply only the flagged changes.

The word "implement" is deliberate. "Rewrite" discards the work built in Step 3. "Implement" keeps changes targeted. Use "implement" in the instructions.

Provide the original dossier and the improvement plan. Implement only the suggested changes. Do not improve beyond what the plan specified. Reproduce the entire dossier with changes made.

## Output

Write the final dossier to `short-story-dossier-[title-slug].md` in the project working directory (or the story-forge project root if running inside the plugin).

Add a one-line header noting: date generated and working title.

Report to the user:
- The selected premise (first sentence only)
- The stated effect
- The magic sword in one sentence
- Output file path
- Next step: "Run `short-story-outline` to map the 8-Point Plot onto scenes."

## Quality Rules

These apply across all generation in this skill:

- No em dashes anywhere in the output. Convert any to commas, colons, or periods.
- No negative parallelism ("not just X, but Y").
- No rule-of-three padding (adjective, adjective, and adjective).
- The effect must be named as a specific emotion or experience, not a category label.
- Every character's heart's desire must be emotionally specific. Functional desires ("she wants to survive") are not heart's desires; emotional desires ("she wants to be the one her mother chose, once") are.
- Maximum six cast members. If a seventh appears, cut one.
- The dossier fits on two pages. If it runs longer, it is carrying novel-weight that belongs in the outline stage.
- No banned words (see `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/references/writing/banned-words.md`). Feed the list as a `<prohibited_words>` constraint at every generation step.

## Operating Notes

- **Do not pad thin input.** If the braindump is sparse, the dossier will be thin. Say so. Do not inflate with genre furniture.
- **This is a dossier, not a bible.** Two pages. The outline stage fills in scene architecture. The draft stage fills in prose. Do not do either job here.
- **Step 4 is critique, not rewrite.** If you find yourself drafting revised dossier content during the critique step, stop. Produce the improvement plan. Apply it in Step 5.
- **Step 5 uses "implement," not "rewrite."** If you catch yourself saying "rewrite the dossier incorporating the suggestions," correct it. Implement is the right verb.
