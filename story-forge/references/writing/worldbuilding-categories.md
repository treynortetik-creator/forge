# Worldbuilding category profile formats

**Every element gets the document structure its category deserves.** A magic system and a god and a
language are not the same kind of object, and giving all three "3-4 sentences of detail" is what turns
a world into a glossary.

Classify the element into one of the categories below, then follow that category's format exactly.
Each opens with a `#` header, carries a single italicised tagline underneath, and uses its own named
`##` subheaders.

**Three structural ideas worth naming**, because they are the ones that generate story rather than
describe it:

- **Hard Rules vs Soft Rules** (magic/technology) — what is *always* true versus what is *usually*
  true and can be bent. *A well-defined system should create story problems, not just solve them.*
- **The Truth Behind the Myth** (gods/deities) — author-eyes-only: what is *actually* true about this
  deity versus what mortals believe. "Unknown" is a legitimate answer.
- **Details to Use in Prose** (culture, languages) — concrete sensory specifics a writer can drop into
  a scene **without stopping to explain them.**

Most categories close with **Narrative Implications**: 3-5 bullets on how this element can generate
conflict, obsession, moral weight or plot momentum, and who might want it, fear it, or want it
destroyed.

## High-level Worldbuilding

For High-level Worldbuilding:

Using the dossier, genre tropes, and author notes as your guide, create a comprehensive overview of this element's foundational rules and identity. Focus on the feel and logic of the worldbuilding element rather than specific locations or systems — this sheet should serve as a north star for all other worldbuilding elements.

Format your response in Markdown as follows:

Begin with a # header with the following format: # High-level Worldbuilding Element: [NAME OF ELEMENT]

Include a single italicized sentence immediately below the header as a "tagline" that captures the essence of this worldbuilding element in one evocative line

Use ## subheaders for each of the following sections, as relevant: Core Premise, What Makes This Element Distinct, Internal Logic & Rules, History.

Under each subheader, write 1-2 sentences of prose. Do not use bullet points within sections — this should read as a document, not a list.

For Setting(s)/Locations:

Create a detailed profile for this specific location that makes it feel lived-in and three-dimensional, not like a stage backdrop. Cover its physical reality and layout, emotional resonance, the people who inhabit it, its secrets, and its role in the larger story.

Format your response in Markdown as follows:

Begin with a # header in the following format: # Setting/Location: [NAME OF ELEMENT]

Include a single italicized sentence immediately below the header as a "tagline" that captures the essence of this place in one evocative line

Use ## subheaders for each of the following sections: Physical Description and Layout, Atmosphere & Emotional Tone, Who Lives Here / Who Passes Through, Purpose & Function in the World, Purpose & Function in the Story, Secrets & Hidden Layers, History & How It Has Changed, Connections to Other Locations

Write each section in prose (1-2 sentences). Avoid bullet points except under Secrets & Hidden Layers, where a short bulleted list is appropriate.

## Objects/Artifacts

For Objects/Artifacts:

Build a full profile for this object or artifact that makes it feel like it has weight, history, and consequence — not just a plot device waiting to be picked up. The most compelling artifacts fit into the world that made them.

Format your response in Markdown as follows:

Begin with a # header in the following format: # Object/Artifact: [NAME OF ELEMENT]

Include a single italicized sentence immediately below the header that captures the essence or reputation of this object in one evocative line

Use ## subheaders for each of the following sections as relevant: Physical Description, Origins & Who Made It, Purpose & How It Works, Costs & Limitations, History & Chain of Ownership, Current Location & State, How It Is Perceived by the World, Myths & Legends Surrounding It

Write each section in prose (1-2 sentences), except Costs & Limitations, which should use a short bulleted list

End with a ## section called Narrative Implications with 3–5 bullets on how this object can generate conflict, obsession, moral weight, or plot momentum — and who might want it, fear it, or want it destroyed

## Magic Systems/Technology

For Magic Systems/Technology:

Detail the mechanics, limits, and costs of this system with enough specificity that a writer could use it consistently without contradicting themselves. A well-defined system should create story problems, not just solve them.

Format your response in Markdown as follows:

Begin with a # header in the following format: # Magic System/Technology: [NAME OF ELEMENT]

Include a single italicized "in one sentence" summary of what this system is below the header

Use ## subheaders for: How It Works, Who Can Access It & Why, Costs & Limitations, Hard Rules (things that are always true), Soft Rules (things that are usually true but can be bent), Social Perception & Power Dynamics, History & Evolution, What Happens When It's Misused

Hard Rules and Soft Rules should be formatted as bulleted lists. All other sections should be prose (1-2 sentences)

End with a ## section called Narrative Implications with 3–5 bullets on how this system can generate conflict, stakes, or character tension

## Groups/Races

For Groups/Races:

Build a full profile for this group/race that treats them as a real community with internal diversity, fractures, and complexity — not a uniform faction or monolith.

Format your response in Markdown as follows:

Begin with a # header in the following format: # Group/Race: [NAME OF ELEMENT]

Include a single italicized sentence immediately below the header as a "tagline" that captures how this group sees themselves or how the world sees them

Use ## subheaders for each of the following sections: Origins, Defining Characteristics, Internal Hierarchy & Social Structure, Core Values & Taboos, Relationships with Other Groups, Greatest Achievements & Deepest Traumas, How They See Themselves vs. How Others See Them, Diversity Within the Group

Write each section in prose (1-2 sentences), except Relationships with Other Groups, which should use a short bulleted list with one line per group

End with a ## section called Narrative Implications with 3–5 bullets on conflicts, tensions, or story opportunities this group presents

## Gods/Dieties

For Gods/Dieties:

Develop a rich, nuanced profile for this divine or supernatural entity. The most compelling gods in fiction are ambiguous — their nature reveals as much about mortals as it does about divinity.

Format your response in Markdown as follows:

Begin with a # header in the following format: # God/Deity: [NAME OF ELEMENT]

Include a single italicized epithet or prayer fragment immediately below the header that captures the essence of this deity in one evocative line

Use ## subheaders for each of the following sections: Domain & Areas of Influence, Origin & Mythology, Personality & Motivations, How They Interact with the Mortal World, What They Want / What They Offer, How Different Groups Interpret Them, Contradictions & Mysteries

Write each section in prose (1-2 sentences)

End with a ## section called The Truth Behind the Myth where you note — for the author's eyes only — what is actually true about this deity versus what mortals believe, even if that truth is "unknown"

## Geography/Nature

For Geography/Nature:

Describe this geographic or natural element in a way that makes it feel like a force with agency, not just scenery. Cover its physical reality, its effect on the people near it, and its deeper significance.

Format your response in Markdown as follows:

Begin with a # header in the following format: # Geography/Nature: [NAME OF ELEMENT]

Include a single italicized sentence immediately below the header that captures the mood or presence of this place or natural feature

Use ## subheaders for each of the following sections: Physical Description & Scale, Climate & Seasonal Patterns, Flora & Fauna, Resources & Dangers, Effect on Cultures & Civilizations Nearby, Spiritual or Magical Significance, How It Is Changing Over Time

Write each section in prose (1-2 sentences), except Flora & Fauna and Resources & Dangers, which may use short bulleted lists

End with a ## section called Narrative Implications with 3–5 bullets on how this natural element can drive plot, create conflict, or shape character

## Population/Politics

For Population/Politics:

Map out the power structures, demographics, and political realities surrounding this element. Political worldbuilding is most useful when it creates pressures that force characters into hard choices.

Format your response in Markdown as follows:

Begin with a # header in the following format: # Population/Politics: [NAME OF ELEMENT]

Include a single italicized sentence immediately below the header that captures the central political tension in one line

Use ## subheaders for each of the following sections: Who Holds Power & How They Got It, Who Is Excluded & Why, Major Factions & Their Agendas, Formal Laws vs. Unspoken Rules, Fault Lines & Potential Flashpoints, How Ordinary People Experience Political Life, Corruption, Reform & Resistance

Write each section in prose (1-2 sentences), except Major Factions & Their Agendas, which should use a bulleted list with one faction per bullet and a brief description

End with a ## section called Narrative Implications with 3–5 bullets on how this political landscape creates story pressure

## Culture

For Culture:

Build out the day-to-day texture of life connected to this cultural element. Culture should feel like something characters swim in unconsciously — not a list of exotic customs bolted onto a plot.

Format your response in Markdown as follows:

Begin with a # header in the following format: # Culture: [NAME OF ELEMENT]

Include a single italicized sentence immediately below the header that captures what most defines this culture's spirit or identity

Use ## subheaders for each of the following sections: Art, Music & Storytelling, Food, Fashion & Material Culture, Core Values & Shame Triggers, Rituals & Life Milestones, How Class, Gender & Identity Shape Cultural Experience, Subcultures & Counter-Movements, Cultural Influence On & From Others

Write each section in prose (1-2 sentences)

End with a ## section called Details to Use in Prose — a bulleted list of 5–8 specific, sensory, concrete details a writer could drop into a scene to make this culture feel real without stopping to explain it

## Languages

For Languages:

Profile this language in terms of its social, cultural, and emotional function — not its grammar. Focus on what speaking or not speaking this language means, and on vivid specific details that make it feel real on the page.

Format your response in Markdown as follows:

Begin with a # header in the following format: # Language: [NAME OF ELEMENT]

Include a single italicized sentence immediately below the header that captures what this language sounds like or feels like to hear

Use ## subheaders for each of the following sections: Origins & Linguistic Family, What Speaking It Signals, Key Untranslatable Words or Phrases, How It Has Changed Over Time, Dialects & Registers, Language as Power or Resistance

Write each section in prose (1-2 sentences), except Key Untranslatable Words or Phrases, which should use a bulleted list with the word or phrase in bold followed by its explanation (aim for 3–5 entries)

End with a ## section called Details to Use in Prose — a bulleted list of 4–6 specific ways a writer could use this language in a scene to convey character, class, or cultural tension without stopping to explain it.


Only include the asked-for worldbuilding details. Do not add any preamble, commentary, or anything other than what I've asked for above. Remember to only use 1-2 sentences for each section, unless stated otherwise.
