## Banned Families (pattern-level, added 2026-07-19)

Extracted from a 131-edit author hand-pass on a full manuscript (2026-07-18). These are FAMILIES, not single strings: sweep the pattern, then judge each hit in voice context before cutting (census, judge, apply — see **draft time doctrine**). Each family carries its exemptions; a blind find-and-replace is malpractice.

- **"Ledger" + order/counting meta-narration.** The word "ledger" is banned outright (now in the list above). The wider family: the telling framing ITSELF as ordered, kept, counted, or accounted ("I'll tell it in order," "the arithmetic of," "do the sums on"). *Exempt:* a character counting concrete, real objects for plot reasons. See the order/ledger meta-tell in `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/references/writing/anti-slop.md`.
- **The chest-cold family.** Emotion or object + "sat/settled/sits" + a body location ("it sat in my chest," "dread settled in her stomach"), and "cold" as an emotion descriptor (cold dread, cold certainty, went cold). *Exempt:* physical cold — weather, ice, a morgue.
- **Held-breath shorthand.** "Held his/her/its breath," "let out a breath," "the room held its breath" as emotional shorthand. Ration to near-zero; one deliberate use per book is a device, recurrence is a fingerprint.
- **Garment-generic "coat."** "Coat" as the model's default garment for set-dressing. Name the specific garment (parka, windbreaker, mittens, scarf) or cut the garment entirely. *Exempt:* garments doing plot work or characterization.
- **Cross-voice idiom contamination** (multi-POV work). Each narrator's exclusive idiom families must stay in their own chapters; a borrowed signature construction is a defect even in a good sentence. Run a both-directions verification pass after every merge or multi-agent edit wave.

## Why These Words Are Banned

The words above represent vocabulary inflation — a measurable AI tell. Post-2022 LLMs pulled heavily from formal writing, academic text, and marketing copy, making these words statistically over-represented in model output. Feeding this list as a `<prohibited_words>` block at the generation step forces the model away from its high-probability lexical defaults toward specific, grounded language.

A related but distinct sub-flavor: clinical, sensor-like verbs standing in for felt physical sensation — *"her fingers registering the cold alloy"* instead of "her fingers touched the cold alloy" or simply "the alloy was cold." "Register/registering" (added above) is the clearest example, flagged in a live model-comparison line-edit (Claude Sonnet 5 draft) as an instant AI-tell: the model describes the body as a data-processing instrument rather than a body having an experience. Watch for the same instinct in other clinical verbs (log, process, catalog, note) applied to touch, smell, or pain.
