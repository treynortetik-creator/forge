# Songs Studied (N=29)

These are the songs behind the `[M-29]` measurements in `emo-pop-punk.md`. We read each lyric through the LRCLIB API for analysis only, and nothing here reproduces any of it. "Title" says where the title sits in the song. BPM comes from songbpm.com where we have it.

## Core era (1999-2008)

| # | Song | Artist | Year | Analysis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | All the Small Things | blink-182 | 1999 | Clipped 4-6 syllable lines with a "na-na" chant section. The title is the song's first line and never reappears in the chorus. Domestic props (stairs, roses). 149 BPM. |
| 2 | Feeling This | blink-182 | 2003 | Every verse line gets a tagged parenthetical answer from a second voice, which is the title. The chorus is two long lines. A late spoken-feeling section opens up the nostalgia. |
| 3 | I Miss You | blink-182 | 2003 | Two-line chorus with the title as a counter-line beneath it. Gothic pop-culture imagery (a horror-film couple, a morgue, spiders) used as romance. 110 BPM. |
| 4 | Welcome to the Black Parade | My Chemical Romance | 2006 | Starts as a slow, piano-led father-and-son story with quoted dialogue, then turns into a march anthem. No conventional chorus, only a "carry on" refrain. The title closes the first verse. |
| 5 | Helena | My Chemical Romance | 2004 | Funeral and weapon props carry a eulogy. Echoed pre-chorus, then a hushed question-and-answer bridge before a doubled final chorus. The title is never sung. 126 BPM. |
| 6 | I'm Not Okay (I Promise) | My Chemical Romance | 2004 | Contradiction hook: the title chanted three times per chorus. A spoken "I'm fine" breakdown flips into a profane final chorus. Props: photographs, a broken foot, a second floor. |
| 7 | Cute Without the E (Cut from the Team) | Taking Back Sunday | 2002 | Two vocalists overlap in the bridge. Accusation built from props (lipstick, collar, window, "clever napkins"). The outro introduces a new hook. The title is never sung. About 95 BPM listed, probably a half-time reading. |
| 8 | MakeDamnSure | Taking Back Sunday | 2006 | Obsessive "you" (33 mentions vs 29 of "I"). The title arrives only late, as an outro hook. A bar, a hangover kit and a bed set the scene. |
| 9 | Sugar, We're Goin Down | Fall Out Boy | 2005 | Prose-like verses with no rhyme scheme crammed into the melody (Stump, Song Exploder). The chorus ends on a multi-word mosaic rhyme. The title is in the chorus, line 2. |
| 10 | Dance, Dance | Fall Out Boy | 2005 | The title opens a 6-line chorus. A pre-chorus built on a coin-and-heart counting image ("two quarters and a heart down"). The writer's own words are part of the hook (meta). 114 BPM. |
| 11 | Thnks fr th Mmrs | Fall Out Boy | 2007 | Sarcastic thank-you hook (title in line 2, twice per chorus) with a quoted taunt inside the chorus. Lines about writing and keyboards make it meta. |
| 12 | Misery Business | Paramore | 2007 | The title is the song's first line. Month and week counts as time stamps and an hourglass/clock prop. The bridge twists through a pronoun swap. The writer later disowned its slut-shaming. 173 BPM. |
| 13 | The Quiet Things That No One Ever Knows | Brand New | 2003 | Long 12-14 syllable chorus lines. The title is buried at the end of verse 1. A short, quiet bridge of repeated confession. Meta line about wasting words. |
| 14 | Sic Transit Gloria... Glory Fades | Brand New | 2003 | Rare all-third-person narrative (he/she) of a first sexual encounter. Strong scene-setting. Chorus switches into first person. The title is never sung. |
| 15 | Hands Down | Dashboard Confessional | 2003 | The most prop-dense song in the set (stereo, lights, hair, clock, gate, door). The title opens a climactic narrative section, not the chorus. About 94 BPM listed, probably half-time. |
| 16 | Vindicated | Dashboard Confessional | 2004 | The title is the first word of the chorus. A self-indicting "I am" list. A chant bridge before the final chorus. A diamond-ring metaphor. |
| 17 | The Middle | Jimmy Eat World | 2001 | Pure second-person pep talk with zero first-person pronouns and almost no props. The title is in chorus line 2. Written in response to a fan email (Songfacts). 162 BPM. |
| 18 | The Taste of Ink | The Used | 2002 | Exact time stamp in verse 1, where the title also sits. Escape-the-town arc. Short screamed/echo answers. About 95 BPM listed, probably half-time. |
| 19 | I Write Sins Not Tragedies | Panic! at the Disco | 2005 | Theatrical scene: a wedding, an overheard bridesmaid, a sarcastic interjection. The most quoted speech in the set. The title is never sung. |
| 20 | Buried a Lie | Senses Fail | 2004 | Gory murder-mystery narrative (grave, scalpel, evidence) used as heartbreak allegory. Short 5-6 syllable lines suited to screams. The title is never sung. |
| 21 | Ocean Avenue | Yellowcard | 2003 | Street names and teenage ages anchor nostalgia. The title is the first line. Slant-rhymed chorus. A bridge with the goodbye memory. 174 BPM. |
| 22 | Best of Me | The Starting Line | 2002 | Long-distance love (phone lines, separate beds). The final chorus rewrites its last line, stepping the resolve down. The title sits mid-chorus. 107 BPM. |
| 23 | My Friends Over You | New Found Glory | 2002 | Blunt thesis: friends before the girl. The title closes the chorus, and the final chorus cuts off before the last word. Very few props. 178 BPM. |
| 24 | Ohio Is for Lovers | Hawthorne Heights | 2004 | Sung/screamed echo pairs throughout. Uses literal self-harm language in the chorus, the cringe and safety case. The title appears only as a variant line. |
| 25 | Jamie All Over | Mayday Parade | 2007 | A road trip dense with place names (Vegas, San Francisco, Georgia, California). Map-and-pen parenthetical answers. The title is never sung. |

## Modern contrast (2011-2021)

| # | Song | Artist | Year | Analysis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 26 | Came Out Swinging | The Wonder Years | 2011 | Hyper-literal adult detail (parents' basement, airports, fast food, "South Philly basement"). Title and gang-vocal hook arrive only in the outro. Meta: songs about getting better. |
| 27 | Your Graduation | Modern Baseball | 2014 | Conversational run-ons, and a years-long time stamp. Shouted counter-vocals in the recording. The last line turns passive regret into a dare. The title is never sung. |
| 28 | December | Neck Deep | 2015 | Title at the end of each chorus half. Perfect-rhyme-led chorus (the minority pattern). Window, stereo and city props. A meta "hear this song" line. |
| 29 | good 4 u | Olivia Rodrigo | 2021 | Contradiction hook bookends the chorus. Receipts (a new car, the therapist, the bathroom floor). The final chorus swaps a wish line for an insult. Proves the 2005 moves still chart. |
